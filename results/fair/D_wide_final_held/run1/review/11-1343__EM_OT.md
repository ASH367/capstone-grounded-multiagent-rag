## Full case as the system saw it (facts)
[1] Beneficiary E.M. Dates of Service: October 1, 2009, to October 31, 2009
[2] The appellant billed Medicare for SNF services provided to E.M., using HCPCS codes RUB02 and RUB03. Exh. 1 at 26. The Medicare contractor determined that the PT, OT, and ST services provided to E.M. were not medically reasonable and necessary, and therefore downcoded the RUG-III codes to reflect the proper RUG­ III codes if the PT, OT, and/or ST services were not medically reasonable and necessary, but the beneficiary received SN services. See E.M. Exh. 1 at 24.
[3] The contractor then denied the downcoded claims as not reasonable and necessary, after finding that the record did not contain documentation to support a finding for medically reasonable and necessary daily SN services. See id.
[4] The appellant contends that the record shows sufficient evidence that E.M.’s physician was aware, ordered, and was in agreement with the therapy plans. See id.
[5] If the physician’s signature is not located within the plan of care, the reviewer may look to other documentation within the record.
[6] The certification and recertification statements may be entered on forms, notes, or records that the appropriate individual signs, or on a special separate form.
[7] In this case, the record contains sufficient documentation that E.M.’s physician ordered the OT services prior to the initiation of therapy services. Specifically, the record contains written orders signed by E.M.’s physician with specific and detailed instructions for OT dated August 21, 2009. See Exh. 2 at 252.
[8] As indicated by the record, OT services were initiated on August 24, 2009 with a treatment diagnosis of lack of coordination and muscle weakness. Exh. 2 at 194. E.M.’s prior level of functional was minimal to moderate assistance with ADLs. Id. at 187. E.M. was referred to OT due to an overall decline in transfers, range of motion, strength, and independence with ADLs. Id. at 187. In September 2009, E.M. required maximal assist and 100% tactical, visual or verbal cue to attend to task. Id. at 186.
[9] The record indicates that during the dates of service, the therapists continued to teach repetitive exercises to improve upper extremity strength to perform ADLs. Id. at 170-176. Further, the record does not indicate that significant functional gains were not made and E.M. remained at a level of extensive to total assist for personal care tasks.
