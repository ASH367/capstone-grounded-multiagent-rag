## Full case as the system saw it (facts)
[1] J.H. was 83 years old and had recently moved in with his daughter when he suffered two falls from which he could not get up and was taken to the emergency room.
[2] The appellant points out that the patient had urinary problems secondary to a history of bladder cancer and prostate hypertrophy which required active physician management, including two emergency room visits to reinsert a Foley catheter, repeated episodes of blood in the urine, and a urinary tract infection. His hypertension medication was changed twice during his stay due to interaction with the urinary problems. Id. The appellant also notes (and the record reflects) that J.H. suffered from severe to profound hearing loss, involuntary movements, and cognitive problems including mental confusion, inability to follow directions, and impulsivity sufficient to call for a one-on-one attendant for nights and evenings.
[3] He also received occupational therapy, focusing on regaining ADLs and developing a home program, each day after July 14, 2004, with evaluation visits and care-planning involvement on the earlier days. Id. at 834. He was also evaluated by a speech- language pathologist and was seen daily for therapy to improve comprehension and expression. Id. at 835-38.
