## Full case as the system saw it (facts)
[1] (Dates of service from September 14, 2004, through November 12, 2004; and from November 13, 2004, through January 11, 2005)
[2] The beneficiary started care with the home health agency on May 17, 2004, approximately four months before the dates of service at issue. F.P.1 File, Exh. MAC-1. Her primary diagnosis was hypertension, and, additionally, coronary atherosclerosis, cellulitus of her leg, asthma, polyarthritis, a duodenal ulcer, and obesity. F.P.1 File, Exh. 13 at 59-60. During the four months in question the beneficiary’s physician certified that she needed skilled nursing services one to two times a month (for observation and assessment of her cardiovascular status) as well as teaching the patient and her caregiver the signs and symptoms of her cardiac condition.
[3] She also received personal care services three to five times a week. Id., see also F.P.2 File, Exh. 13 at 56-57.
[4] The contractor denied Medicare coverage on redetermination on the ground that it was not reasonable or medically necessary to provide monthly skilled nursing visits to assess longstanding medical problems when there had been no acute changes in the beneficiary’s condition or treatment. F.P.1 File, Exh. 3 at 15; F.P.2 File, Exh. 3 at 15.
[5] On reconsideration, the Qualified Independent Contractor (QIC) found that the documentation provided was inadequate and that the beneficiary’s care had not met Medicare coverage criteria. F.P.1 File, Exh. 9 at 31. Although the beneficiary had reported “heart palpitations,” the record did not indicate that the home health agency had thoroughly assessed the cause of the palpitations. Id.; see also F.P.2 File, Exh. 13 at 51-52. The beneficiary was also noted to have lower extremity edema, but it was not quantified or further assessed. Id. The beneficiary was also noted to have wheezing in her lungs, but there was no record whether she was compliant in her nebulizer/inhaler treatments. Id.
[6] Lastly, the QIC noted that there had been no changes in the beneficiary’s medications or plan of care; in addition, no skilled teaching was documented. Id. The QIC concluded that the home health agency was responsible for the noncovered costs. F.P.1 File, Exh. 9 at 31-32; see also F.P.2 File, Exh. 9 at 31-32.
[7] Moreover, there is no evidence that the signs and symptoms that were recorded in the nurses’ notes prompted further investigation, a consultation with the physician, additional teaching about medications, or other steps to treat the beneficiary. See F.P.1 File, Exh. 13 at 54, 55; F.P.2 File, Exh. 13 at 51, 52.

## What each system said (the answer is NOT the gold label; the reviewer decides)
### Eligibility agent: SUPPORTS_COVERAGE
- Skilled nursing observation and assessment is a type of service the policy covers. A physician certified the need for it, so the type-of-service and certification conditions appear met. Medical necessity, homebound status and documentation adequacy are not settled here and are left to other review stages.  [passage 236: "Observation and assessment of the patient's condition by a nurse are reasonable and necessary skilled services"]
- Skilled teaching of the patient and caregiver is also a recognized covered service category, provided the teaching and the responses are documented.  [passage 245: "the teaching services and the patient/caregiver responses must be documented"]
### Medical-necessity agent: DOES_NOT_SUPPORT
- Continued skilled observation and assessment requires the home health record to document a rationale for a reasonable potential of complication or acute episode. The case record shows no follow-up on the recorded signs and symptoms.  [passage 236: "must document the rationale that demonstrates that there is a reasonable potential for a future complication or acute episode"]
- Signs such as edema and respiratory changes may justify skilled observation. Here, the edema was noted but not quantified or further assessed, so it does not establish the need.  [passage 236: "edema, symptoms of drug toxicity, abnormal/fluctuating lab values, and respiratory changes on auscultation may justify skilled observation and assessment"]
- Skilled teaching is covered only when the teaching services and the patient/caregiver responses are documented. The QIC found no skilled teaching documented despite the physician's certification of teaching.  [passage 245: "the teaching services and the patient/caregiver responses must be documented"]
### Documentation agent: DOES_NOT_SUPPORT
- The policy requires the home health record to document a rationale showing a reasonable potential for a future complication or acute episode to justify skilled observation beyond 3 weeks. The case record shows the QIC found documentation inadequate and the nurses' notes show no follow-up on recorded signs and symptoms, so the required rationale is absent.  [passage 236: "Information from the patient's home health record must document the rationale that demonstrates that there is a reasonable potential for a future complication or acute episode"]
- Edema and respiratory changes may justify skilled observation under the policy, but the record shows the edema was not quantified or assessed and there was no record of nebulizer/inhaler compliance. The documentation needed to use these signs as justification is therefore missing.  [passage 236: "Such signs and symptoms as abnormal/fluctuating vital signs, weight changes, edema, symptoms of drug toxicity"]
- Teaching services and the patient/caregiver responses must be documented. The physician certified teaching of cardiac signs and symptoms, but the QIC found no skilled teaching documented.  [passage 245: "the teaching services and the patient/caregiver responses must be documented"]
- Policy requires documentation of the current medical condition at every visit and support for the need for continued skilled nursing, even for a stable patient. The record shows no evidence that recorded signs and symptoms prompted any investigation, physician consultation, or treatment steps, so this support is lacking.  [passage 233: "at every home health visit, the patient’s current medical condition must be described and there must be documentation to support the need for continued skilled nursing services."]
### Orchestrator: NOT_COVERED
Skilled observation and assessment and skilled teaching are covered service types, and the physician certified them. However, the record lacks the required rationale for a reasonable potential of complication or acute episode. The edema, palpitations and wheezing were not assessed or followed up, and no skilled teaching was documented. The Medical Necessity and Documentation agents therefore prevail over the Eligibility Agent, whose support depended on those unsettled issues. The denial applies to both periods.
### Single agent: NOT_COVERED
The record shows no reasonable potential for a change in treatment from the monthly skilled observation, no documented skilled teaching, and no follow-up on recorded signs and symptoms. The case facts do not establish that the skilled nursing services were reasonable and necessary.
